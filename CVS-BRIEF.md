## 1 · What these two do together

BOI's officer is deciding whether to trust a twelve-month software programme to these people. The two CVs are halves of one answer: **Justin covers the commercial and operational half, Dmytro the engineering half.** They must look like a matched pair from one company, not two documents from different decades.

**The style: a professional profile, not a résumé.** Confident, spare, evidence-led. Every claim earns its place by being relevant to *this* project. A CV that lists everything is weaker than one that proves one thing.

---

## 2 · Shared structure for both

| Zone | Content |
|---|---|
| **Header band** | Name large · role · one-line positioning. Justin gets his photograph; Dmytro uses initials unless he supplies one |
| **Identity strip** | Contact and nationality details, small, tabular, immediately beneath the header |
| **Profile** | Three or four sentences. The person in summary — no padding |
| **Experience** | Reverse chronological. Role · organisation · dates · location, then two or three lines of substance |
| **Capability panel** | The bridge to this project — why this history matters *here*. This is the section that does the persuading |
| **Education / credentials** | Brief |

Use the same grid, type scale, and section rhythm for both so they are unmistakably a pair.

## 5 · Craft notes

- **Dates in a tabular column**, right-aligned or in a fixed left gutter, so the career reads as a continuous record at a glance. Both CVs show unbroken timelines — let the layout show it.
- **One accent per page.** Pink for the current role and the single strongest figure.
- **No skill bars, no percentage ratings, no icons for their own sake.** Those read as junior. Plain, well-set type is stronger.
- **The capability panel is the payload** — give it real space, not a footer strip. It is where a government officer decides these people can deliver.
- **Footer:** thin gradient rule, `Education AI Group Co., Ltd.` left, page number right.
