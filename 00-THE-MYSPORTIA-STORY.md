## How it started

The founders noticed something about their own lives: sport was everywhere at school and university,
and then it simply stopped. They looked into it and found the number — **three in four people stop
playing sport entirely after they leave education.**

The reason was not that people stopped wanting to play. **It was that access broke down.**

The moment that crystallised it: **it took over two hours of phone calls to organise a single game
of paintball.** A venue with no online booking, a group to coordinate, no way to see availability.
Meanwhile a flight, a hotel or a car could be booked in ninety seconds.

Sport is one of the last major consumer categories with no unified way in. Travel has one.
Accommodation has one. Transport has one. Food has one. Sport does not.

---

## The insight that shaped the entire company

The obvious build was a consumer marketplace — somewhere to find and book any sport. The founders
started there, and hit a wall immediately.

**The venues were the bottleneck.** They ran on notebooks, spreadsheets, phone calls and LINE
messages. No real-time availability, no digital payments, no central system. **You cannot build a
marketplace on top of businesses that are not bookable.**

> **Booking.com built the hotel extranet before it built the marketplace.**
> Hotels first got booking systems. Only then did a booking marketplace become possible.

So the order was inverted, deliberately:

### **First, fix the venues. Then build the thing people actually use.**

That decision is the spine of the whole story, and it is what the BOI application is describing.

## The three phases

This is the storyline. Everything else is detail hanging off it.

### Phase 1 — Fix the venues *(the work that is already done)*
Give every sports and activity business a single system that runs it: bookings and schedules,
payments, invoices, receipts and VAT, memberships and packages, customer records, staff and
resources, automated reminders. Replace the notebook and the spreadsheet. Make the venue
**instantly bookable**.

This is unglamorous, and it is the part that makes everything after it possible.

### Phase 2 — The marketplace *(partly built, being completed)*
Once venues are digitised, aggregate them. One place to find any sport or activity by location, date
or type, see real-time availability, book and pay in a few taps. In Thai and English. For residents
and for the 39 million people who visit Thailand each year.

**The network effect that makes this work without marketing spend:** every venue that joins brings
its existing members with it. They become marketplace users on day one. In the pilot, a handful of
venues brought tens of thousands of users into the system with no consumer advertising at all.

### Phase 3 — The lifestyle layer *(this is what the BOI project builds)*
A booking engine gets someone to one session. It does not change whether they still play in a year.
**What changes that is having someone to play with, something to play for, and a reason to come back.**

- **Player profiles and skill ratings** — a record of what you play and how you are progressing
- **Opponent and partner matching** — by sport, skill level, location and availability, so a person
  with no group can still play
- **Leagues, tournaments and rankings** — anyone can create a league; fixtures, results, standings
  and national rankings by sport
- **Activity feed and community** — follow venues, trainers and other players
- **Native mobile applications** for players and for venue operators

The company's own framing of the full journey:

> **Discovery → Booking → Participation → Community → Progression**

Phase 1 and 2 cover discovery and booking. **Phase 3 is participation, community and progression —
and that is the half that actually reverses the three-in-four number.**

## How this maps onto the BOI application

This is the point. The 15/85 split is not an arbitrary percentage — it is exactly this story.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The 15% that is built** | **Phase 1 and the booking layer of Phase 2.** The venues are fixed. They are running on the system at 32 pilot venues in Thailand, taking bookings and payments |
| **The 85% this project builds** | **Phase 3 — the lifestyle layer** — plus what national scale requires: venue hardware integration, automated tax reporting to the Revenue Department, AI-assisted onboarding, and a multi-tenant backend |

**Say it this way on the slide, because it is true and it is far stronger than a feature list:**

> We spent the first year fixing the venues, because nothing else was possible until they were
> bookable. That work is done and running. **What this project builds is the half that changes
> whether people actually play** — matching people to opponents, running leagues and rankings, and
> putting it in their hands on mobile.

A reviewer assessing whether there is real new development here can see the difference immediately.
The first half is plumbing. **The second half is the product.**

## The problem, with the evidence

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sedentary population** | Over **75% of Thai adults are highly sedentary** — sitting more than seven hours a day |
| **The drop-off** | **3 in 4 people stop playing sport entirely** after school or university |
| **Why** | Not lack of interest. **Lack of access and convenience** |
| **Fragmentation** | Venues run on phone calls, LINE chats, paper schedules and spreadsheets. No central place to discover or book anything |
| **Retention** | Having someone at your level to play with is the single biggest predictor of whether a person keeps participating |
| **Minority sports are invisible** | Climbing, diving, Muay Thai, archery, community leagues — happening across the country, with no way for the people who would play them to find them. Empty courts and underused facilities |
| **No visibility for the state** | A largely cash sector means no participation data, no sector picture, and tax revenue left uncollected |

## The opportunity

**As work becomes more sedentary and more virtual, real physical experience becomes more valuable,
not less. Sport is the antidote to digital fatigue.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Sports tourism in Thailand | **2.5 million international tourists** already take part in sports activities, generating over **€423 million** |
| Visitors annually | **39 million+** |
| Venues with no online booking | **Over 60%** |
| Government campaigns this supports | **Amazing Thailand Grand Tourism and Sports Year**; **National Physical Activity Strategy 2018–2030** |

Active travellers stay longer and spend more, and that spending spreads to small towns rather than
concentrating in the tourist centres.

---

## What Thailand gets

- **Higher participation** in sport and physical activity, against a measured 75% sedentary rate
- **Amateur and minority sports made visible** — once the connective layer exists, the marginal cost of carrying one more sport is close to zero
- **Venues brought into the digital tax system** through automated reporting to the Revenue Department, at no cost to the state
- **Sports tourism** supporting the government's own stated objectives
- **Skilled Thai technology employment**, and the training that goes with it
- **Participation data** the state has never had — which sports are growing, where, and among whom

**This voice and the BOI register agree with each other.** "Inevitable, not promotional" is exactly
the right tone for a government reviewer. Use it.

## 🔴 What must NOT carry across from these documents

The source material was written for investors and for a ministry, not for BOI. Several things in it
would damage the application.

| | In the source documents | What the application says |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **"5% commission" / "take rate"** stated as the model throughout | **Tiered monthly software licence fee**, fixed by band, set independently of venue revenue. *Por.12/2566 cl.5.2.1 excludes commission and intermediary income from qualifying revenue — this is the single most dangerous phrase in the investor pack* |
| **2** | **"Free, no subscription"** | The licence model is the opposite. Free-plus-commission is the framing BOI rejects |
| **3** | **Exsportia** as the venue-side brand | **MySportia** throughout |
| **4** | Founding dates of **2021 / 2022**, pilots in **2024** | Education AI Group was incorporated **17 September 2025**. Ignore all dates in the source material |
| **5** | **Dmitri Muzika** named as a co-founder | Only **Justin Cohen** and **Dmytro Bodlev** are named in the pack |
| **6** | **"Thailand's Official Sports Marketplace"**, "in association with the Thai government", MoU language | **There is no signed government MoU.** The company's own claims register forbids this |
| **7** | **40,000 users · 14 venues · 3,000+ businesses listed · 60% conversion · 90% retention · one venue +40% revenue** | Investor-pack figures, not reconciled to the BOI timeline and internally flagged as awaiting evidence. **The application states 32 pilot venues.** Do not mix the two sets |
| **8** | International expansion, global rollout, "LinkedIn of sport" | The application is about **building the product in Thailand**. Scale ambition stays out |

**Everything else — the problem, the founding insight, the three phases, the architecture, the
network effect, the national benefit — transfers directly and is the strongest narrative material
the company has.**
